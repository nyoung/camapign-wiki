# Session 35 — The Heist of the Heist

## Summary
[[Ganav]] rejoined the party and was caught up — including on a wanted poster offering **three gold crowns** for [[Pilpoul Ivri]], which [[Hildegarde]] had quietly torn down to protect him. Inside **[[Madame Reitenbach]]**'s tenement, the party gently walked back the news they'd delivered at the end of [[Session 34 - I Believe You Dropped Your Hat|Session 34]]: the Garden of Mórr body was **not** her son. Fragile hope replaced grief, and she gave them the most valuable lead yet — on his last visit, about a week before, **[[Gerd Breytenbach]] had mentioned helping out a questionable group he called "the Lurkers."** She also named a maid he'd been close to, **[[Louisa May]]**, and described a bookish son taught to read by a dock-worker father who died in the **Red Pox of '02**.

Two blocks away the party scouted the **[[Shallyan Orphanage]]** for the first time — a fortress in all but name, granite-walled, every window barred, ringed by an eight-foot spiked iron fence anchored into the stone road. Finding no way in, they settled on infiltration by invitation: Hildegarde would present herself as a childless woman of a certain age looking to volunteer, or half of a couple looking to adopt.

That evening, in street clothes, they descended on **[[The Pig and Tack]]** — a riverside tavern two-thirds of which juts out over the water on a chaotic accretion of lumber, pitch-black inside and hostile to conversation. Hildegarde talked her way into a card game with a well-dressed gambler called **[[Fiedler]]**, playing deliberately badly while probing about smuggling someone out of the city; [[Boris Dorchen|Boris]], drunk off a single pint, bought a round for the entire bar and accidentally thawed the room. Out of the darkest corner stepped **[[Anselm Rathsky]]** — not a frontman but the **leader of the [[Black Cloak Lurkers Below]]** — who gave his account of the gang war: the Lurkers had stolen the lottery takings **from the [[Steel Fist Thief Ring|Steel Fists]], who (he claimed) had already stolen them from the Prince**. The heist of the heist. Now the Steel Fists are hunting every Lurker they can find, and Rathsky is bleeding men. His offer: **stop the Steel Fists' pursuit, and he'll get someone out of the city.** *(An hour later [[Captain Crais]] told them something different — see Loose Threads.)*

The next morning brought errands and a deadline. At a tailor's, Hildegarde commissioned a cape for Boris and learned the Steel Fists' identifier — a **flat cap in a distinctive red-orange** — then bought a pale cap to dye and use as bait or an opener. At the temple plaza, **[[Reiner Kolber]]** delivered the urgent news: **[[Elfrieda Teuschel]] has cut a deal with a barge captain at the far north end of the city and means to flee at sundown**, just before the river nets go up. Finally, at the City Watch, **[[Captain Crais]]** heard Berin's report that the Breytenbach body was a plant, and offered grim news of his own — embalmed and long-decayed corpses drifting downriver toward the docks. [[Ganav]] landed the session's sharpest observation: the **[[Howling Ones]]** mark at [[Schraeder's House|Schraeder's]] was so badly drawn that no true cultist would own it. Deliberate misdirection.

## Events
- **[[Ganav]] rejoined** the party and was brought up to speed. A **wanted poster for [[Pilpoul Ivri]]** (three gold crowns, sought by the Empire) had been quietly removed by [[Hildegarde]] to protect him, given his ties to the Verenans
- The party confirmed what they had suspected since [[Session 33 - Plot Twist|Session 33]]: the morgue body **does not match** [[Gerd Breytenbach]]'s description
- **Interviewed [[Madame Reitenbach]]** in her tenement. Told gently that the body might not be her son's, she moved from wailing grief to cautious hope. She revealed:
  - Gerd was quiet and bookish, and frequented booksellers near the **Temple District**
  - His father was a **dock worker who taught him to read** and died in the **Red Pox plague of '02**
  - On his last visit, roughly a week prior, Gerd mentioned **helping out a questionable group he called "the Lurkers"** — a comment clearly weighing on him
  - He had been close to a maid named **[[Louisa May]]**, who worked in the same household
- The party donated what food they had and left. Hildegarde paused at the door to listen for movement inside and heard only silence. Ganav briefly suspected the mother of concealing something, then revised his view — her grief had been too convincing
- **Scouted the [[Shallyan Orphanage]]** (formally *The Hospital for the Maintenance and Education of Exposed and Deserted Children*), two blocks away: three storeys of solid granite, all windows barred, an eight-foot spiked wrought-iron fence anchored into the stone road, and a weathered statue of Shallya in the courtyard, her outstretched hand still holding the faint shape of a dove. Hildegarde found no gaps and no climbable sections. Plan settled on: **enter by invitation** — Hildegarde as a childless volunteer drawn to Shallya, or as half of a couple looking to adopt
- Changed out of the loaned Myrmidian robes into common street clothes and went to **[[The Pig and Tack]]**. Roughly twenty pairs of eyes and as many weapons turned on them at the door; the cigar-chewing dwarf barman made clear the house was for drinking, not talking
- **Hildegarde ran a card game** — ale downed, widest smile, pack of cards — drawing in a well-dressed gambler with a frilly collar, shaped goatee and sharply angled hat known only as **[[Fiedler]]**, whose crew emerged from the shadows to play. Stakes climbed to two shillings an ante. Playing deliberately poorly, she probed him about smuggling someone out of the city; Fiedler warned that the gang war had made leaving nearly impossible, and that **barges caught in the crossfire had burned on their way downriver**
- **[[Boris Dorchen|Boris]], drunk off a single pint** of the rock-gut ale, bought a round for the entire bar — eight shillings badly spent and a hostile room considerably warmed
- **[[Anselm Rathsky]] emerged from the darkest corner**, betrayed only by the cherry of his cigar, and introduced himself as **leader of the [[Black Cloak Lurkers Below]]**. Charmed into candour by Hildegarde, he gave his version of the gang war: the Lurkers **stole the Prince's lottery takings from the [[Steel Fist Thief Ring|Steel Fists]] after the Steel Fists had already stolen them** — the heist of the heist. The Steel Fists are now hunting every Lurker they can find and his organisation is bleeding. **His offer: do something to stop the Steel Fists' pursuit, and he can get someone out of the city.** Contact runs through the bartender
- Returned to the **[[Tree and Rut Inn]]** through moonlit streets, Berin steering the group along better-lit routes. Folk musicians played "The Reik is Wide" to a singing common room. Berin fished for gang-war rumours among the patrons and got only vague nods; Hildegarde asked the innkeeper for a reputable tailor
- **Next morning:** Hildegarde slipped out early to a tailor on the north side of the river, commissioning a **custom cape for Boris** — fifteen shillings, four-day turnaround. She and Berin drew out the tailor on the Steel Fists' style: the gang is identified by a **flat cap in a particular red-orange**, and wearing one by accident in the wrong neighbourhood is dangerous. Hildegarde bought a light-coloured cap for three shillings to dye to match — bait for the Steel Fists, or an opener
- At the **temple plaza**, [[Reiner Kolber]] waited by a statue of a mounted soldier (someone had skewered a gourd on the sword's tip). His news: **[[Elfrieda Teuschel]] has made a deal with a barge captain docked at the far north end of the city and plans to flee at sundown**, just before the river nets are raised. He also confirmed the **Steel Fists' main territory is the southwest tenements along the city wall**, and warned that priestly garb earns little trust there after dark. Berin paid him and set him to keep watching Teuschel's residence and send word to the inn by day's end
- At the **City Watch**, **[[Captain Crais]]** received the party's update. Berin reported that the body thought to be Gerd Breytenbach was likely a **plant** — its wounds suggesting a scuffle rather than the precise professional killing that took the other victims. Crais shared in return:
  - **Embalmed bodies and long-decayed remains** have been found floating downriver *from the direction of Wolfenburg*, and corpses discovered wrapped around the city's docks
  - The **Steel Fists** ran an illegal lottery scheme recently cracked down on by the Prince; the **Lurkers** specialise in thievery, lockpicking, and fencing through untraceable channels
  - He has **no knowledge of anyone named Astrid**, nor of recent [[Howling Ones]] activity in the city; a recent caravan led by [[Gromril van Aelst]] reported the roads entirely clear of them
- **[[Ganav]]'s observation:** the mark of the [[Howling Ones]] found at [[Schraeder's House|Jaie Schraeder's apartment]] was so poorly executed that no self-respecting member of the cult would have claimed it — clear evidence of **deliberate misdirection** by someone covering their tracks

## Notes / Loose Threads
- **⏳ Hard deadline: [[Elfrieda Teuschel]] flees by barge at sundown**, from the far north end of the city, just before the river nets are raised. The party has most of a day
- **⚠️ Two accounts of the lottery money, and they don't match.** [[Anselm Rathsky|Rathsky]] said the [[Steel Fist Thief Ring|Steel Fists]] had *stolen* the takings from the Prince before the Lurkers took them — "the heist of the heist." [[Captain Crais]], an hour later, said the Steel Fists **ran** the illegal lottery themselves and the Prince merely cracked down on it. Crais has no stake in the story; Rathsky was mid-pitch, recruiting. The party has both versions on record and has not yet noticed the discrepancy
- **Gerd Breytenbach was working with the Lurkers.** His mother's offhand detail ties the missing manservant directly to [[Anselm Rathsky]]'s gang — and Rathsky was standing in front of the party without either side raising the name. Does Rathsky know where Gerd is?
- **The Howling Ones mark at Schraeder's was faked** by someone who had never seen the real thing — reinforcing the frame-up, and narrowing the suspect pool to outsiders imitating the cult
- **Embalmed and long-decayed corpses are drifting downriver** and collecting at the docks — a strong echo of the desecrated-corpse plot from [[Beeckerhoven]] and the disturbed corpses at the [[Temple of the All Gods (Wolfenburg)|Temple of the All Gods]]
- **[[Louisa May]]** — Gerd was close to her, and she died in the fire. Whether that closeness made him a target, a witness, or a conspirator is unresolved
- **Barges burn in the crossfire** of the gang war, per Fiedler — a complication for any river escape, including Teuschel's
- **Crais has never heard of Astrid** and reports no Howling Ones activity in Wolfenburg, while [[Astrid Hofhaimer]] confronted Hildegarde inside the city in [[Session 32 - A Tooth For a Tooth|Session 32]]. Either the Watch's intelligence is poor, or her presence is being kept off the books
- **⚠️ Naming discrepancy — Reitenbach vs Breytenbach:** GM Assistant's transcript for this session consistently renders Gerd's mother as "Madam **B**reitenbach," matching her son's surname, where [[Session 34 - I Believe You Dropped Your Hat|Session 34]] recorded her as "Madame Reitenbach." The Session 34 spelling may have been a dropped initial consonant. Left as-is pending the GM's ruling — see [[Madame Reitenbach]]
