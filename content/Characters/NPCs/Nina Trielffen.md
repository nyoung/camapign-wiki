# Nina Trielffen

## Description
A **small** woman who works at the [[Temple of Shallya (Wolfenburg)|Temple of Shallya]], first seen pushing a mop over a mess left by a patient. Berin remarked that people are sometimes just small.

## Role / Occupation
A **lay servant**, not a sister: *"I just clean and help organize, but I don't take care of the sick."* She volunteers and works for the temple.

## Personality
Welcoming, a little watchful. She kept an eye on what passed between the two Hildes while [[Berin Garibaldi|Berin]] talked theology at her.

## Relationships
- **[[Hilde Muntz]]** — a friend: she brought the party to her, hugged her goodbye, and said *"I hope that they know what they're doing and can get you out of here safe."* Hilde went to her *"to let Nina know before we go"*
- **[[Marla Hengist]]** — the orphan girl who clings to Hilde, in the same ward
- **[[Gammer Isolde Ulricht]]** — her superior; away at the Cathedral of Sigmar for the day's ceremony

## History with the Party
**[[Session 40 - A Wonderfully Thought-Out, Half-Baked Plan|Session 40]]:** Asked the lost-looking party who they wanted, told [[Berin Garibaldi|Berin]] that Gammer Isolde was at the Cathedral of Sigmar, and led them to [[Hilde Muntz]]. **[[Hildegarde]] noticed that she positioned and twisted her hand oddly as she spoke**, a gesture she couldn't read, and suspected it was a signal to the [[Steel Fist Thief Ring|Steel Fist]] watchers outside. [[Berin Garibaldi|Berin]] noticed nothing. Berin kept her talking for several minutes on the theology of Shallya and Myrmidia while Hildegarde spoke with Hilde. She led the group down to the basement laundry. She winced at the bedsheets Berin knotted into a rope.

## Known Information
- Hildegarde suspected a hand signal (unconfirmed): see [[Christina Asper]]'s Session 30 signals with a tattooed initiate
- She did not seem to know about the plan beforehand, but accepted it readily: *"good"*
- Her question to Berin: *"Why the interest in the laundry?"* was never really asked; she took them there without fuss

## Open Questions
- Was that a **signal** — and to whom? Are the Steel Fists' watchers being fed information from inside the temple?
- Is she **the tattooed initiate**'s contact, or just a coincidence of gesture?

## [[Session 41 - Too Warm for Me|Session 41]]
- **Hilde names her as the one who hid [[Gerd Breytenbach|Gerd]].** *"That was Nina. Nina was the one who brought him there and was hiding him."* Hilde knew him from before and told no one
- **Not gang-affiliated.** A **follower of Ranald**, *"an honest thief"* — a *principled thief*: no murder or thuggery, and no robbing the poor
- **The hand-sign was not a signal to the watchers**, or not necessarily: it fits **Ranaldian thief-signs**. Hilde taught [[Hildegarde]] a **sequence** that tells Nina that *Hilde is safe and these people are trustworthy*
- *Open question closed (probably):* the odd hand movement in [[Session 40 - A Wonderfully Thought-Out, Half-Baked Plan|Session 40]] — Hildegarde's suspicion that she signalled the Steel Fists — now has an innocent reading
## [[Session 43 - A Mighty Gift|Session 43]]
- **Met the party in the courtyard**, hugged [[Marla Hengist|Marla]] and scolded her, then took the group to [[Gammer Isolde Ulricht]]. [[Hildegarde]] gave her Hilde Muntz's hand-sign; she answered with a nod and led them through the temple's quiet halls, checking for followers
- **She distrusts [[Hermann Roeging]]**: *"I don't think he's just working for the Shallyans."* She knew the party only through Hilde Muntz and Asper
- **Brought them to [[Gerd Breytenbach|Gerd]]** and vouched for them: *"These are friends of Hilde Muntz"*
- **Berin's Blessing of Conscience:** [[Berin Garibaldi|Berin]] told her, in private, that Gerd bore Nurgle's taint and that she must warn Gammer. She was shocked, steeled herself, and agreed. She added that Gerd had been a friend to many thieves and had been tending wounded Lurkers when his household was taken
- **Her honest-thief reading holds:** she shelters the weak but has no love for the Steel Fists and the Lurkers' murderers
